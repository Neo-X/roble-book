The final project lets students apply learned skills to an area of interest while digging deeper into the concepts. Projects do not need to use real robot hardware, but there are options to obtain hardware, and you are encouraged to investigate a problem that controls something in the real world.

Projects are **in groups of 2 or 3 students**. Each project starts with a proposal to ensure the right scope.

**Example project ideas:**
- Re-implement a method in a new deep-learning framework
- Re-implement a method from a paper that did not release code
- Choose a robot and train a model to make it solve a task (e.g., walking)
- Investigate a new method that improves exploration
- Investigate a new method for learning improved representations
- Study Sim2Real by learning and transferring policies to another simulation or real hardware
- Perform a literature review of Sim2Real papers

**Grading:** Students are graded together but may receive different grades.

### Real Robot Hardware

To use real hardware for your project, include the requested hardware in your project proposal. The course has budget allocated to purchase hardware.

### Team Expectations

- Commit code to Git frequently (active at least 3 days/week, 1+ commits each day).
- Attend all group meetings, or explain why and make a reasonable effort to catch up.
- Contribute to discussions clearly, effectively, and respectfully.
- Respond to all relevant group communications within one day.
- Agree to reasonable amounts of work and deadlines. Keep the team informed about challenges.
- Actively seek to complete an equal share of the work.

**Suggestions for groups:**
- Find a good meeting mode — in-person or [Discord](https://discord.com/) / [Slack](https://slack.com/) for online collaboration.
- Respect everyone's mode of communication and task preferences.
- Use a Scrum master to ensure everyone has a voice.

### Handling Group Problems

If you feel a group member is not contributing, include "DROPPED THE BALL" in your confidential iteration evaluation. If a student is found not contributing well, the professor will:

1. Email the whole team highlighting the situation.
2. Meet with the team or individual members to outline required changes.
3. An under-performing student may receive a reduced grade (~50%) for that iteration.
4. An under-performing student may be placed on **probation**.

**Probation:** A student on probation must demonstrate improved behaviour within the probation period (usually one iteration). If they remain unproductive, they may be removed from the group.

Students removed from groups will either receive 0 on remaining work or must complete it individually, with a likely 25% penalty for failure to function in the team.
